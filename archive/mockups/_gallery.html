e-paper companion · friction probes

Three input bets, one device

The e-paper companion is the product; these are friction probes — laptop stand-ins for how the device will behave, built to feel the frictions before committing to hardware. The one that matters: after cooking, how do you tell it what you used? Each probe answers differently. Full write-up in mockups/README.md.

How to read this: eink-companion is the canonical device (the baseline). The three companion-* probes each carry the same cooking flow but attack the post-cook pantry update differently — voice, prediction, photo. eink-glance is the retired broadsheet, kept for its visual grammar. Thumbnails are live but frozen; click Open for the real thing (keyboard-driven — each page lists its keys). To feel the whole loop in a probe: Cook → tap through → finish → rate → update the pantry.

My take — before living with them

These are meant to be lived with, not judged from a gallery — but a prior: run the encoder control first, honestly. If a pre-ticked "used tonight?" checklist that's right most of the time feels fine, you've saved a mic, an audio pipeline, and a latency problem — and the answer to "do we need to hear you" is no. That's the cheapest possible win and it should be disproven before it's dismissed.

Voice is the one to want — saying "I'm out of X" is the most human version and the best for dirty hands — but it carries the most firmware risk and the only round-trip the user has to wait on. Worth it only if the encoder control actually feels worse in the kitchen.

Photo is the long shot. Its real value here is forcing the question the other two dodge: maybe the pantry isn't edited on the device at all — the phone sees, the chicken confirms. Keep it as a sketch until that question gets pressure.

The unlock: all three end on the same confirm screen. Build that once, well, and the input bet becomes a swappable front-end — which is exactly the kind of decision you want to defer until the kitchen tells you.
